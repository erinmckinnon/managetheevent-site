Most projects sit in one lane. This one connected three: a nonprofit party I was planning, a beer brand with a Halloween campaign to launch, and the staffing agency that puts brand ambassadors in the field. Because I was working in all three, one sponsorship turned into a full Halloween program across Tampa Bay.

## The Connection

I organized and planned the 13 Ugly Men Foundation’s Halloween Party at the historic Cuban Club in Ybor City. At the same time, New Belgium was rolling out Voodoo Ranger Halloween, a “Party Without Fear” campaign built around its Slasher IPA and a Halloween video game promotion.

The fit was obvious. I brought Voodoo Ranger in as a sponsor of the party, which gave the foundation a strong brand partner and gave Voodoo Ranger a big, costumed crowd in exactly the mood its campaign was built for.

## Building the Program

The party was one stop. Through Michele & Group, I booked and managed the brand ambassadors for a Halloween activation series across Tampa Bay, so the campaign reached very different crowds in the same week:

- **The Canopy at the Birchwood**, a rooftop bar in St. Petersburg
- **Crabby Bill’s, Indian Rocks Beach**, two nights at the beach
- **Zoo Tampa**, during its Halloween event
- **South Bar Tampa**, a packed college bar
- **The 13 Ugly Men Halloween Party** at the Cuban Club

Ambassadors worked 13 shifts in branded jumpsuits, sampling Slasher IPA and Juicy Haze, handing out Halloween swag and driving sign-ups for the video game and a cooler raffle.

![](/uploads/work/voodoo-halloween-crabby-tent.jpg)

## The Results

- **6 activations** at **5 accounts** from October 23 to 31
- **2,060 consumers** engaged face to face
- **1,020 samples** poured
- **13 talent shifts** booked, staffed and recapped

## Wins Worth Repeating

**A new account lead.** At Crabby Bill’s, the owner tried both beers and wanted to start carrying Voodoo Ranger. Our ambassador got his contact information to the brand’s rep the same night.

**Converting non-IPA drinkers.** At Zoo Tampa, guests who said they don’t like IPAs were won over by Juice Force and Juicy Haze, and one asked to buy it right at the tent.

**A new, younger audience.** South Bar’s 21+ college crowd, many of them trying an IPA for the first time, took the most samples of the program.

**Swag that worked.** Jumpsuits, bandanas, Polaroids and a 1960s living-room set at the Cuban Club gave people a reason to stop, take photos and stay.

![](/uploads/work/voodoo-halloween-13um-lounge.jpg)

## Closing the Loop

When the program wrapped, the brand asked for the top-line numbers: accounts activated, samples, audience size and big wins. I reviewed all 13 field recaps and more than 200 event photos, edited the best ones, and delivered a branded recap presentation covering results by activation, event-by-event highlights, what consumers said, and recommendations for next year.

The recap was honest about what to improve, too. The biggest lessons were to lock in sampling-friendly placement and match shift hours to when crowds actually peak, and to use bigger promo signage in loud venues so the game sells itself.

## Why It Works

When the person planning the event, connecting the sponsor and staffing the field all sit at the same table, nothing gets lost between handoffs. The nonprofit gets a sponsor that fits. The brand gets more than a logo on a banner: it gets a program, real consumer feedback and a recap it can hand straight to leadership.
