Jose Cuervo Sparkling was authorized at hundreds of 7-Eleven and Speedway stores across Florida, but authorization doesn't put cans in a cooler. Many stores had never placed an order. Some didn't know the product was approved. Others had it stuck in the back room or listed incorrectly in their ordering system.

Proximo needed people in those stores, talking to the right managers, and they needed to know exactly what happened at every stop. So I built a field program designed around one idea: nothing happens that we can't see.

## The Challenge

A program like this lives or dies on follow-through. The brand needed:

- Reps who would actually reach each store's decision maker, not just drop by
- Proof of every visit, with photos and consistent answers
- A clear picture of why stores weren't ordering, store by store
- Fast handoff of orders and issues to the distributor
- Live results the client could check without waiting on a report

And it had to work across the entire state, from Jacksonville to the Keys.

## The Solution: A Tracked Field Program

I mapped the full store list into routes by geography, so each rep could cover a tight cluster of stores efficiently. Then I built the system around a simple, enforced process:

### 1. Call first

Before visiting, reps call every store on their route to confirm it's open, confirm it's actually a 7-Eleven or Speedway, find out who the buyer is, and schedule the visit. No call logged, no paid visit. That one rule eliminated wasted drives and surfaced closed or mislisted stores before anyone got in a car.

### 2. Visit and fix

At each store, reps check shelf and cooler placement for each SKU, look for product in the back room, confirm how it's listed in the store's ordering system, put product out where they can, and work with the manager to place an order.

### 3. Report everything

Every visit ends with a structured recap: exterior and in-store photos, placement for each SKU, ordering status, barriers to ordering, and any follow-up needed. Recaps are reviewed before a store is marked complete.

## What I Built

**A field rep dashboard.** Each rep gets a personal route page with their stores, maps, a call script, and links to log each call and visit, plus a training guide with a self-check quiz they must pass before starting.

**A Q&A board.** Reps post questions to a shared board and published answers help everyone on the program, instead of the same question coming in by text twelve times.

**Live route tracking.** Every route shows calls logged, visits done, reports approved, orders secured, and follow-ups flagged. Routes that should have started but haven't light up automatically, and reps get reminder emails they confirm with one tap.

**Client results and a weekly follow-up report.** The client and distributor see results store by store, add their own notes, and work from one shared follow-up report each week, so every order and issue gets handled.

## Early Results

The program launched with a test group of reps in October 2026 to refine the process before a full rollout. In the first weeks:

- 730 stores mapped into 90 routes across 12 regions
- 262 pre-visit calls logged
- 187 store visits completed and reported
- 35 orders secured, with managers ordering on the spot, committing to order, or confirming the product was already on its way
- District and store managers requesting in-store tastings after their first shipment

Just as valuable: the program surfaced exactly why stores weren't ordering, from delivery issues to system listings, so the brand and distributor could fix the problems behind the numbers.

## Why It Works

Most field programs tell you a rep went to a store. This one tells you what the rep found, who they talked to, what changed, and what still needs to happen. Every step is visible to me, the client, and the distributor.

The structure is built to scale, and it isn't limited to retail. The same tracked model works for off-premise sampling programs or any activation where a brand wants real accountability for every stop.
